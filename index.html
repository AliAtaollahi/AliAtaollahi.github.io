Short Biography

I am a Computer Engineering graduate from the University of Tehran. My research lies at the intersection of programming languages, formal models, and dependable systems. I am interested in techniques that turn precise theory into practical verification tools.

My current work includes adaptive programming with Marjan Sirjani and collaborators, and extending MoXIchecker with Dirk Beyer and the SoSy-Lab team. Across these projects, I study how verification can scale and remain useful as systems change.

Research Projects

Adaptive Programming

I study when verification knowledge can be reused safely after a program or model evolves. In our work on timed actor systems, my tasks have included:

  • Co-designing a slice-equivalence criterion that connects static program analysis to weak timed bisimulation and safe tiny-twin reuse.
  • Building AdaptChecker end to end: parsing Timed Rebeca, constructing dependence graphs, computing observable backward slices, checking graph isomorphism, and producing diagnostics and visualizations.
  • Resolving mismatches between formal definitions and examples, adding compatibility checks and 48 regression tests, and evaluating ten model revisions.

Extending MoXIchecker

I contribute to extending MoXIchecker as a versatile symbolic-model-checking platform. My work has focused on:

  • Implementing and optimizing IC3/PDR components and improving IC3IA correctness, invariant handling, bit-vector counterexamples, generalization, propagation, and BMC lookahead.
  • Adding direct MoXI input, subsystem flattening, modular verification engines, and fixes for QF_BV parsing and model loading.
  • Building reproducible BenchExec studies to answer how theories, SMT solvers, interpolation backends, encodings, and input formats affect BMC, k-induction, IMC, ISMC, IC3, and IC3IA.
